# Analytic limits

A drift-kinetic solver can agree with another code and still share its errors.
Analytic limits are the independent check: asymptotic results derived from the
equation itself, which a correct discretization must approach as its
parameters approach the limit. This page lists the limits DKX is tested
against, what was measured, and which limits remain open.

## Limits verified in the test suite

| Limit | Reference | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spitzer–Härm conductivity, full Fokker–Planck, $Z = 1, 2, 4, 16$ | Spitzer & Härm 1953 | within 0.36 % |
| Lorentz conductivity, thermal factor $8/\sqrt{\pi}$ | Braginskii 1965 | 1.7e-13 |
| Pfirsch–Schlüter approach, $O(\epsilon^2/\nu^2)$ | Helander & Sigmar 2002 | observed order 1.99 |
| Shaing–Callen / Boozer–Gardner collisionless bootstrap coefficient | Shaing & Callen 1983 | $\sqrt{\nu}$ approach, 4 % at $\nu' = 10^{-4}$ |
| Onsager symmetry $L_{ij} = L_{ji}$ of the thermal transport matrix under refinement | Onsager 1931 | asymmetry falls at least 3× per rung, to 7e-5 |
| Zero tokamak particle flux with like-particle Fokker–Planck collisions | Helander & Sigmar 2002 | 1.6e-8 of $L_{22}$ |
| Fokker–Planck conservation of density, momentum and energy | Rosenbluth et al. 1957 | 1e-15 |
| Monoenergetic-to-thermal convolution with a manufactured $D^*$ | Beidler et al. 2011 | 1.5e-15 |

The tests are `tests/test_physics_limits.py`, `tests/test_transport_limits.py`,
`tests/test_shaing_callen.py` and `tests/test_collision_physics_gates.py`. Each
asserts the tolerance in the table, so a regression in any of these limits
fails CI.

The conservation and convolution rows are identities of the discrete operator,
so they hold to round-off. The Spitzer–Härm, Pfirsch–Schlüter and Shaing–Callen
rows are asymptotic, so what is tested is the approach: the error falls at the
predicted rate as the parameter moves toward the limit. The physics behind each
operator is in {doc}`../physics/collisions` and {doc}`../physics/reduced_models`.

## The collisionless bootstrap limit

```{figure} ../_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/shaing_callen_convergence.png
:alt: Bootstrap coefficient approaching the Shaing-Callen collisionless limit as collisionality falls.
:width: 85%

Monoenergetic bootstrap coefficient on the W7-X standard configuration at
$r_N = 0.5$, for $\nu'$ from 1 down to $3\times10^{-4}$ at $E^* = 0$ and
$E^* = 0.003$, against the Shaing–Callen value computed by `dkx.shaing_callen`.
Generated by `tools/paper_benchmarks/shaing_callen_convergence.py`; the data are in
`docs/_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/shaing_callen_convergence.json`, which
also records the resolution used at each point ($N_\theta = 29$,
$N_\zeta = 55$, $N_\xi = 64$ at the collisional end).
```

The Shaing–Callen coefficient is a geometric integral over the flux surface.
The finite-collisionality $D_{31}^*$ approaches it with a $\sqrt{\nu}$
boundary-layer correction. That approach, not agreement at one collisionality,
is the check: low collisionality needs pitch-angle resolution that grows as
$\nu$ falls, so the resolution schedule is part of the artifact.

The companion artifact
`docs/_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/low_collisionality_validity.json` records
where the local, monoenergetic model stops being valid at low collisionality
(Hinton & Hazeltine 1976; Shaing 1984; Ho & Kulsrud 1987). It is a regime map on
a modest $17 \times 31$ angular grid, not a converged-coefficient benchmark.

## Collisionality trends

```{figure} ../_static/figures/docs/transport_coeff_vs_collisionality.png
:alt: Transport coefficients against collisionality across the 1/nu, plateau and Pfirsch-Schlueter regimes.
:width: 80%

Transport coefficients against collisionality across the $1/\nu$, plateau and
Pfirsch–Schlüter regimes.
```

The three low-to-high collisionality regimes are distinguishable in the
monoenergetic $D_{11}^*$: a $1/\nu$ rise in a stellarator at $E^* = 0$, a
plateau, and a collisional tail. A finite $E^*$ removes the $1/\nu$ branch by
$E\times B$ detrapping. Rung `examples/04_monoenergetic_scan` checks two of
these trends on every run: $D_{11}^*$ must be monotone in $\nu'$ at $E^* = 0$,
and $D_{33}^*$ must approach its collisional value of 1 from below.

The SFINCS 2014 collisionality figures for LHD and W7-X are reproduced from
`tools/publication_figures/generate_sfincs_paper_figs.py`, with full Fokker–Planck
and pitch-angle-scattering operators side by side:

Ion transport matrix elements against collisionality, W7-X geometry,

## The high-collisionality limit is not closed

The Simakov–Helander high-collisionality limit is the one analytic limit of the
SFINCS 2014 paper that DKX does not claim. Momentum-conserving collision
operators should approach inverse-$\nu$ scaling of $L_{11}$ and $L_{12}$ for
$\nu' \gg 1$, while pitch-angle scattering scales like $+\nu$. Two checked
artifacts record where this stands:

- The **trend proxy** (`bounded_proxy`) fits power-law tails to the audited LHD and
  W7-X collisionality scans. The LHD Fokker–Planck tail satisfies the loose
  inverse-$\nu$ proxy on the checked range; the W7-X tail does not yet
  (`tools/publication_figures/artifacts/dkx_high_collisionality_trend_proxy_summary.json`).
- The **normalization audit** (`bounded_proxy`) confirms that the checked
  `sfincsOutput.h5` files carry every Appendix-B geometry ingredient, recomputes
  `FSABHat2` from `BHat` and `DHat`, and keeps the reproduction closed because
  the scans stop near $\nu' = 10$
  (`tools/publication_figures/artifacts/dkx_simakov_helander_limit_audit_summary.json`).

```{figure} ../_static/figures/paper/dkx_simakov_helander_limit_audit.png
:alt: Simakov-Helander high-collisionality normalization and readiness audit.
:width: 85%

Simakov–Helander readiness audit. The run plan that would close it, a
logarithmic extension of both scans to $\nu' \approx 100$, is
`tools/publication_figures/artifacts/dkx_simakov_helander_high_nu_run_plan.json`.
```

The full reproduction is recorded as `closed_deferred` in the
{doc}`validation_matrix`. The run plan's first LHD Fokker–Planck pilot at
$\nu' = 17.78$ took about 262 s on two RTX A4000 GPUs and about 345 s on one on
the explicit solve path, with the three right-hand-side residuals at 4.33e-16,
5.33e-14 and 4.06e-11; the implicit-differentiation path took about 569 s for
the same point. One transport point per several minutes makes the full
extension a workstation campaign rather than a CI check.
