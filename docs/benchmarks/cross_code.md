# Cross-code agreement

SFINCS parity ({doc}`sfincs`) tests whether DKX reproduces one discretization of
the drift-kinetic equation. Independent codes with independent discretizations
test something else: whether both are converging to the same continuum answer.
The overlap between codes is narrower than any one code's feature set, so each
comparison on this page states the equations it covers.

```{figure} ../_static/figures/readme/cross_code_validation.png
:alt: Left, DKX against SFINCS v3 scaled error by case. Right, DKX against MONKES and YANCC relative difference by device and coefficient, with the 6 percent gate.
:width: 92%

Left: DKX against SFINCS v3 on matched decks, scaled error 1e-10 to 1e-8; the
W7-X row converts to physical flux units through the native path and agrees to
0.3%. Right: DKX against MONKES and YANCC on the monoenergetic coefficients,
with the 6% release gate. Every number is read from the sealed artifacts under
`validation/` by `python tools/benchmarks/cross_code_readme_figure.py`.
```

## MONKES and YANCC

The comparison artifact is `validation/independent_cross_code_v1.json`. It
covers the equation every participating code shares: zero-field monoenergetic
drift kinetics with Lorentz pitch-angle scattering and DKES trajectories. Three
devices span the geometry range:

| Device | Reference code | Maximum relative difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DSHAPE (axisymmetric tokamak surface) | YANCC | 5.51% |
| NCSX | YANCC | 1.66% |
| W7-X EIM | MONKES database, at its authored $27 \times 55 \times 140$ resolution | 5.52% |

All four coefficients pass the 6% acceptance criterion, and $D_{33}^*$ differs
by at most 0.065%. The threshold covers the measured cross-discretization
spread, is fixed by the accepted artifact, and cannot be relaxed to admit a
later regression.

The comparison does not equate similarly named inputs. The audit maps physical
$\nu/v$ through DKX's applied `nuDHat(x0)` factor, converts the dimensional DKES
coefficients to the Beidler $D^*$ convention with the local surface radius,
corrects the reference-field convention, and applies the handedness map to
$D_{31}^*$ and $D_{13}^*$. It recomputes all of this from raw coefficients:

```console
python tools/paper_benchmarks/audit_independent_cross_code_validation.py --yancc-root ../YANCC
```

The artifact pins external commits, input and output checksums, reference
residuals, resolution, hardware, wall time and peak RSS. It is not a
full Fokker–Planck, finite-$E_r$, ambipolar-profile, experimental, or
performance comparison. KNOSOS overlap and low-collisionality trend comparison
with MONKES are recorded as open entries in the {doc}`validation_matrix`.

## The ICNTS monoenergetic benchmark

The International Collaboration on Neoclassical Transport in Stellarators
(Beidler et al. 2011) compares codes on $D_{11}^*$, $D_{31}^*$ and $D_{33}^*$
against collisionality at several normalized fields $E^*$.

```{figure} ../_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/monoenergetic_icnts_w7x.png
:alt: D11 and D31 against collisionality on the W7-X standard configuration at several E*, with SFINCS v3 points.
:width: 85%

Monoenergetic $D_{11}^*$ and $D_{31}^*$ on the W7-X standard configuration at
$r_N = 0.5$, for $\nu'$ from 3e-3 to 30 at $E^* = 0, 0.03, 0.1$, at
$(N_\theta, N_\zeta, N_\xi) = (29, 55, 64)$. Boxes are SFINCS v3 on matched
decks. Generated by `tools/paper_benchmarks/monoenergetic_icnts_w7x.py`; data
in `docs/_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/monoenergetic_icnts_w7x.json`.
```

The same scan on the TJ-II standard configuration at $s = 0.493$, the
strong-ripple end of the benchmark set, uses a Boozer $|B|$ spectrum through
`geometryScheme = 13` (`tools/paper_benchmarks/monoenergetic_icnts_tjii.py`,
data in `monoenergetic_icnts_tjii.json`), and an HSX record is in
`monoenergetic_icnts_hsx.json`, both beside the W7-X data.

## Trajectory models and collision operators

SFINCS 2014 compared DKES, partial and full trajectory models across $E_r$.
DKX reproduces those sweeps with the same upstream switches:

```{figure} ../_static/figures/paper/dkx_er_trajectory_sweep_tokamak_reference.png
:alt: Fluxes and bootstrap current against normalized radial electric field for DKES, partial and full trajectory models on a tokamak.
:width: 85%

Tokamak $E_r$ sweep across DKES, partial and full trajectory models; the models
agree exactly at zero field and separate at finite field. A `release_ready`
entry of the {doc}`validation_matrix`; the stellarator companion
`dkx_er_trajectory_sweep_stellarator_fast_reference.png` is a
`regression_scaffold`.
```

## Ambipolar field and impurities

These two figures are physics results rather than code-to-code agreement; they
are listed here because they are the benchmark cases most often compared across
neoclassical codes.

```{figure} ../_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/w7x_ambipolar_er.png
:alt: Radial current against electric field on W7-X, with every sampled evaluation and the ion, unstable and electron roots.
:width: 85%

Radial current against $E_r$ on W7-X with every sampled evaluation and the
classified ion, unstable and electron roots
(`tools/paper_benchmarks/w7x_ambipolar_er.py`). A literature comparison against
a reconstructed W7-X discharge is `closed_deferred` until its provenance is
pinned ({doc}`validation_matrix`).
```

```{figure} ../_static/figures/paper_benchmarks/impurity_transport.png
:alt: C6+ impurity flux against collisionality on W7-X.
:width: 85%

Neoclassical and classical C6+ impurity flux on the W7-X standard
configuration (`tools/paper_benchmarks/impurity_transport.py`). The script
anchors the neoclassical flux to the Fortran v3 golden output of
`quick_2species_FPCollisions_noEr` and checks the classical flux of
`dkx.impurity` against its `classicalTransport.F90` counterpart to machine
precision.
```
