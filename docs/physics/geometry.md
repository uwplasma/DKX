# Magnetic geometry

The model is local to one flux surface, so the geometry enters only as coefficients on the
$(\theta,\zeta)$ grid: the field strength $\hat B$ and its angular derivatives, the
Jacobian factor $\hat D$, the covariant and contravariant components
$\hat B_\theta, \hat B_\zeta, \hat B^\theta, \hat B^\zeta$, the flux functions
$\hat G$, $\hat I$, $\iota$, and, for some terms, radial derivatives and the metric
$|\nabla\hat\psi|^2$. Every source produces the same frozen container,
`dkx.magnetic_geometry.FluxSurfaceGeometry`, which mirrors the SFINCS v3 `geometry.F90`.

| 11 | Boozer `.bc`, stellarator-symmetric | Boozer | `from_boozer(...)` via `read_boozer_bc` | `format = "boozer"` |

`kinetic_operator_from_namelist` accepts `geometryScheme` 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 11, 12 and 13;
scheme 13 needs at least one `boozer_bmnc` or `boozer_bmns` amplitude in
`geometryParameters`. A native Boozer file is read by `read_native_boozer`, which detects
the column convention (scheme 11 or 12) from the file.

## Analytic schemes

Scheme 1 is the three-helicity model of `geometry.F90`:

$$
\hat B = \hat B_0\Bigl[1 + \epsilon_t\cos\theta
  + \epsilon_h\cos(l\theta - n\zeta)
  + \epsilon_a\sin(l_a\theta - n_a\zeta)\Bigr],
$$

with `NPeriods = max(1, helicity_n)`. The namelist defaults
(`FluxSurfaceGeometry.from_scheme`) are $\epsilon_t = -0.07053$, $\epsilon_h = 0.05067$,
$\epsilon_a = 0$, $(l, n) = (2, 10)$, $\iota = 0.4542$, $\hat G = 3.7481$, $\hat I = 0$,
$\hat B_0 = 1$. Setting $\epsilon_h = \epsilon_a = 0$ gives an axisymmetric,
large-aspect-ratio tokamak.

Schemes 2, 3 and 4 use the fixed harmonic tables of the ICNTS benchmark (Beidler et al.,
Nucl. Fusion 51, 076001, 2011):

| Scheme | Field periods | $\iota$ | $\hat G$ | $\hat B_0$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 LHD standard | 10 | 0.4542 | 3.7481 | 1.0 |
| 3 LHD inward | 10 | 0.4692 | 3.6024 | 1.0 |
| 4 W7-X standard | 5 | 0.8700 | $-17.885$ | 3.089 |

(`_SCHEME_CONSTANTS` in `src/dkx/magnetic_geometry.py`.) Analytic fields carry no radial
dependence, no radial derivatives and a zero $|\nabla\hat\psi|^2$ placeholder, so they
cannot be used with magnetic drift schemes or classical fluxes.

```{note}
The native alias `tokamak` builds scheme 1 with the default parameters above
(`_geometry_context` in `src/dkx/execution.py`), which include the $l=2$, $n=10$ helical
term $\epsilon_h = 0.05067$. It is therefore a three-helicity model field, not an
axisymmetric tokamak. The same field is used on every listed surface; only the radial
conversion factors change with $\psi_N$.
```

## Boozer representation

In Boozer coordinates the field strength is a Fourier series,

$$
\hat B(\theta,\zeta) = \sum_{m,n}\hat B^c_{mn}\cos(m\theta - N_p n\zeta)
                     + \hat B^s_{mn}\sin(m\theta - N_p n\zeta),
$$

where $N_p$ is the number of field periods and $n$ does not include it (the Fortran
`boozer_bmnc(m,n)` convention). The sine terms are present only without stellarator
symmetry (scheme 12). The covariant components reduce to the flux functions,
$\hat B_\theta = \hat I$ and $\hat B_\zeta = \hat G$, and $\hat D$ follows from
$\hat B^2/(\hat G + \iota\hat I)$.

The retained harmonics are those the grid can represent, as in the v3 assembly:

$$
0 \le m \le \left\lfloor N_\theta/2\right\rfloor,
\qquad
|n| \le \left\lfloor N_\zeta/2\right\rfloor,
$$

with the Nyquist exclusions for sine and cosine pairs. The retained set therefore changes
with resolution, and so do the geometric coefficients and the trapped-passing structure.

For `.bc` files the loader interpolates between the two bracketing surfaces and computes
the radial derivatives and $|\nabla\hat\psi|^2$ that magnetic drifts and classical fluxes
need.

## VMEC

For `geometryScheme = 5` the equilibrium is a VMEC `wout` file, netCDF or the LIBSTELL
text form (`dkx.vmec_ascii.read_vmec_wout_ascii`, selected by file signature). The loader
interpolates the requested surface with the v3 half/full-mesh finite-difference
conventions (`vmec_radial_interpolation`, `VMECRadialOption`), filters modes
(`min_Bmn_to_load`, `VMEC_NYQUIST_OPTION`, `RIPPLESCALE`, `HELICITY_N`), and reconstructs
$\hat B$, its derivatives and $|\nabla\hat\psi|^2$ in VMEC angles. Stellarator-asymmetric
equilibria (`lasym = T`) are read with their complementary-parity tables (`bmns`, `gmns`,
`bsubumns`, ...) and included through a separate mode filter
(`_from_vmec`, `src/dkx/magnetic_geometry.py`).

The toroidal flux at the boundary, $\hat\psi_a$, keeps the sign of the file
(`psi_a_hat_from_wout`). {doc}`normalizations` explains why reported $\hat r$ fluxes are
outward-positive regardless.

A JAX-native equilibrium code can feed the same formulas; the finite-beta example
`examples/vmex_finite_beta/finite_beta_vmec_to_sfincs.py` builds a `wout` with `vmex` and
scans $E_r$ on several surfaces.

## Differentiable geometry

`from_scheme` (schemes 1-4) and `from_fourier` (scheme 13) are pure JAX and can be traced
by `jit` and `grad`. `from_fourier` is the geometry entry point for optimization:
gradients with respect to `bmnc` and `bmns` flow through $\hat B$, $\hat D$ and the
flux-surface averages. Grid truncation is applied by zeroing amplitudes so shapes stay
static. `examples/autodiff/differentiable_geometry_gradients.py` checks these gradients.
VMEC and `.bc` readers are NumPy file parsers and are not differentiated.

## Container fields

`FluxSurfaceGeometry` follows the `sfincsOutput.h5` names:

| Field | Output name |
| --- | --- |
| `b_hat`, `db_hat_dtheta`, `db_hat_dzeta` | `BHat`, `dBHatdtheta`, `dBHatdzeta` |
| `d_hat` | `DHat` |
| `b_hat_sub_theta`, `b_hat_sub_zeta` | `BHat_sub_theta`, `BHat_sub_zeta` |
| `b_hat_sup_theta`, `b_hat_sup_zeta` | `BHat_sup_theta`, `BHat_sup_zeta` |
| `g_hat`, `i_hat`, `iota`, `n_periods`, `b0_over_bbar` | `GHat`, `IHat`, `iota`, `NPeriods`, `B0OverBBar` |
| `gpsipsi` | `gpsiHatpsiHat` ($\lvert\nabla\hat\psi\rvert^2$) |

`vprime_hat` and `fsab_hat2` compute $\hat V'$ and $\langle\hat B^2\rangle$.

## Not supported

- No Miller or other shaped-tokamak parameterization. Tokamak studies use scheme 1 with
  $\epsilon_h = 0$, a Boozer file, or a VMEC equilibrium.
- `force0RadialCurrentInEquilibrium = .false.` is refused; DKX assembles the current-free
  form of $\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{B}$ and the $E_r$ terms (`src/dkx/inputs.py`).
